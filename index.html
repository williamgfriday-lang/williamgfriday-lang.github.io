The five most common barriers

  1. Unlabeled page sections — 1,288 instances. Content outside labeled landmark regions. Page content placed where screen-reader navigation cannot reach it in order.
  2. Text too faint to read — 1,230 instances. Weak color contrast. Text that low-vision visitors physically cannot read.
  3. Links that say nothing — 317 instances. Links with no usable name. A screen reader announces “link” — and nothing else. The visitor cannot know where it goes.
  4. Scrambled headings — 211 instances. Heading structure out of order. Headings are how a blind visitor skims a page, and when they jump around out of sequence, the page stops making sense.
  5. Images with no description — 173 instances. Missing image alt text. An image with no description is invisible to a screen reader — the visitor hears nothing where the picture was meant to speak.

What this means

These are not design opinions. Each barrier is a place where a blind or low-vision customer — in a metro area where thousands of residents live with vision loss — cannot complete the task the website exists for: booking, buying, applying, asking. Every barrier counted here is fixable. Most are inexpensive to fix. They persist because nobody has measured them — until now.

Questions, answered straight

Can AI alone make my website accessible?

No. Automated scans and AI are good at the mechanical layer — missing image descriptions, unlabeled buttons, weak contrast. They cannot tell you whether a blind customer can actually finish your checkout, booking, or contact form with a screen reader and a keyboard. That takes a person.

Can a scan or an audit guarantee my site is compliant, or stop a lawsuit?

No — and no honest company can promise that. Braille Bridges finds and explains accessibility barriers and ranks what to fix first. That is evidence about your site, in plain language. It is not a legal shield, and we will never sell it as one.

How is Braille Bridges different from an accessibility widget?

A widget is code installed on your website that adjusts pages inside the visitor's browser. It does not change your site itself, and the barriers stay in your code. Braille Bridges installs nothing. We test your actual website the way a blind customer uses it — by ear, by keyboard, with a screen reader — and report what we find.

What does the free first look include?

We scan the first page of your website — the page most customers see first — and send you a plain-language list of the barriers we found, plus a recording of the findings. No card, no catch, no obligation. It is one page only; the rest of your site is not checked at this tier.

What can't an automated scan see?

A scan reads code. It cannot tell whether a checkout can be completed start to finish with a screen reader, whether a keyboard gets trapped in a menu or pop-up, whether a form's error messages are announced or silent, or whether a PDF can be read at all.
